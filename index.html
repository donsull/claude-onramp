1. Register for Claude

Sign in with your MIT login so your account is covered by the course.

Steps

  1. Open your web browser and go to claude.ai.

  2. Use the email box, not the Google or Apple buttons. Type your MIT email address and click Continue with email.

    The blue box shows where to click. The dashed box shows what not to use.

  3. On the MIT sign-in page, type your Kerberos username and click Next.

  4. Finish the MIT sign-in, including the second sign-in step on your phone if asked.

  5. You should land on the Claude chat screen.

Use your MIT Enterprise workspace. Open the account menu in the bottom-left corner and check that Massachusetts Institute of Technology Enterprise is selected. If you also have a personal Claude account with the same email, you will see both Personal and MIT Enterprise. Always choose MIT Enterprise for your course work.

Done when

If something goes wrong

I was asked to create a personal account or pay

You probably used Google, Apple, or a personal email. Go back and type your MIT email in the email box.

The sign-in step on my phone never arrives

This is MIT's sign-in, not Claude. Check that your phone has signal and that the Duo Mobile app is open. If it still does not arrive, contact the STS Help Desk at stshelp@mit.edu or 617-253-2000.

It says I do not have access

Your account may not be set up yet for Claude Enterprise. Contact the STS Help Desk at stshelp@mit.edu or 617-253-2000, and copy the TAs at ta_15900_fall2026@mit.edu so they know.

4. Create the project and connect your folder

A project keeps your course folder, instructions, and chats together. Connect only the course folder you just created.

Two names to remember. Your project is called Competitive Strategy. Your main folder is called Competitive Strategy Cases.

Before you start, check that Massachusetts Institute of Technology Enterprise is selected in the account menu, in the bottom-left corner. Keep your course materials in this workspace.

Steps

  1. In the Claude app, click Projects in the menu on the left.

  2. Click the black New project button, at the top right of the page.

  3. In the box Name your project, type Competitive Strategy.

    The blue boxes show where to click in this window: the name, Use a folder, Private, and Create project.

  4. Click Use a folder and choose your Competitive Strategy Cases folder. This connects the folder on your computer to the project.

  5. Leave Visibility on Private. Do not choose the MIT option, which lets everyone in your organization see the project.

  6. Click Create project.

  7. Check that it worked. In the project, start a chat. Check that Cowork is selected in the message box, next to the +, and not Chat.

    Then send this message:

    What is in this folder?

    Claude should say that the folder is empty.

Do not connect your whole Documents, Downloads, or Desktop folder. Keep the Claude app open while you work.

Done when

If something goes wrong

I do not see the New project button

Update the Claude app to the latest version and open it again.

I chose the MIT option for Visibility

Open the project and click Share, to the right of the project name. Under General access, click the line that starts with Everyone at and choose Only people invited.

I connected the wrong folder

Open the project page. Under Folder, click the X next to the folder name to remove it from the project. This does not delete the folder from your computer. Then click the + next to Folder and choose the right folder.

Claude cannot reach my folder

Open the Claude app again. Claude can reach your folder only while the app is open.

Chat vs project

Both live in the same Claude app and use the same account. The difference is what each one can see and do.

The short version

A chat is a conversation. You type, or upload files, and Claude answers. A project is a workspace with its own files, instructions, and memory. When you connect a folder to a project, Claude can read the files there, create files, and work through a task in steps.

Side by side

ChatProject with a folder
Where you start itNew, in the left menuProjects, in the left menu
What it works withWhat you type and the files you upload to the conversationThe folder you connect, with all the files in it, plus the project's instructions
What it producesAnswers in the conversation, and artifacts shown next to itFiles saved in your folder, such as web pages, charts, and cleaned data
Your filesIt works on the copies you uploadIt can change files in your folder, with your approval
How it worksOne message at a timeSeveral steps in a row, and it shows what it did so you can check
SetupSign in and startSign in, open the app, create a project, and connect a folder (guides 1 to 5)

You can use both in the same course. For example, ask a chat to explain a concept, then use your project to run the analysis.

Choosing a model

Claude comes in different models. They trade depth for speed, so the best choice depends on the task.

What a model is

A model is the engine behind Claude. Every message you send is answered by one model. Different models reason at different levels and answer at different speeds.

The trade-off

  • More capable models handle harder reasoning, longer files, and multi-step work better. They are slower and use more of your usage allowance.
  • Faster models answer quickly and cost less. They fit simple, well-defined jobs.

Claude's main models come in tiers: Haiku is the fastest, Sonnet is in the middle, and Opus is the most capable.

Which model for which task

A good starting point:

TaskStart with
List, rename, or reformat filesA faster model
Summarize a document or draft a short noteA balanced model
Clean messy data, plan an analysis, or work through many stepsThe most capable model
Double-check a result you doubtThe most capable model, and check it yourself too

A bigger model is not always right. Check the results whichever model you use.

How to switch models

  1. In the Claude app, use the model selector near the message box.

Artifacts

An artifact is something Claude makes for you, such as a document, a chart, or a web page.

In a regular chat, artifacts appear in a panel next to the conversation. In a project with a folder, Claude can also save files in your folder, and you open them yourself.

How to ask for one

  1. Say what you want and what to call it.

  2. Say where to save it. A folder called outputs keeps your results apart from the original files.

  3. Open the file from your folder yourself.

  4. Ask for one change at a time.

  5. Save each version under a new name.

You can ask for other formats too, such as Excel, PDF, or Word. Dashboards lists them.

Exercises

R1. Make a first artifact

Ask
Create a one-page summary of the main points in these documents as a web page called summary.html. Save it in a new folder called outputs.
Check
Open the outputs folder and double-click summary.html. It opens in your browser. Does it say anything that is not in the documents?

R2. Change one thing

Ask
Change only the title and the first paragraph of summary.html. Leave everything else the same.
Check
Open the file again. Was anything else changed?

Safety tips

Claude can read, change, and delete files in the folders you connect. A few habits keep your work safe.

  • Work in a dedicated folder. Connect only a folder that holds your course files, not your whole Documents, Downloads, or Desktop folder.
  • Keep the originals. Work on a copy and leave the original file untouched.
  • Read before you approve. By default, Claude asks for your approval before it changes files or takes actions. Read each request. If you do not understand it, say no and ask Claude to explain.
  • Say what not to touch. When you are unsure, add this to your message: Do not delete or overwrite any files.
  • Keep private information out. Do not type passwords or personal information into Claude, and do not save them in files in your folder.
  • Use your MIT Enterprise workspace. Claude may run your tasks in the cloud, so the files it uses can leave your computer. Your MIT Enterprise workspace has MIT privacy and security protections, and a personal Claude account does not. Open the account menu, in the bottom-left corner, and check that Massachusetts Institute of Technology Enterprise is selected.
  • Know the rules for course data. Read MIT's guidance on data and AI tools: IS&T's AI Tool Guidance, Risk Classifications, and Navigating Data Privacy.
  • Check the results. Claude can make mistakes. Check numbers and quotes against the source before you use them.
  • Sign out on shared computers. Sign out of Claude when you finish.
